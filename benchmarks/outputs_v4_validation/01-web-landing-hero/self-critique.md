**Contrast math** (WCAG relative-luminance formula, verified with a small Python script, not eyeballed):

- L(`#12161f`) = 0.00801, L(`#94a3b8` row text) = 0.35951.
- `#506197` (new `.selected` bg): L = 0.12404 → contrast vs `#12161f` = **3.00:1** (passes 1.4.11's ≥3:1). Text `#94a3b8` on this bg = **2.34:1**.

**Blocking finding — constraint 1 (≥3:1 bg-vs-ambient) and constraint 2 (≥4.5:1 text-vs-new-bg) are mathematically incompatible for `.selected` given the fixed ambient `#12161f` and fixed text `#94a3b8`, and this was proven rather than assumed before finalizing.** WCAG contrast ratios telescope exactly through a shared +0.05 constant: for any candidate background X with luminance between the ambient's (0.00801) and the text's (0.35951),
`contrast(text, ambient) = contrast(text, X) × contrast(X, ambient)`.
Here `contrast(text, ambient) = (0.35951+0.05)/(0.00801+0.05) = 7.059`. Simultaneously satisfying `contrast(X, ambient) ≥ 3` and `contrast(text, X) ≥ 4.5` would require a product ≥ 13.5 — nearly double the fixed 7.059 ceiling. Candidates outside that luminance band fail too: X darker than the ambient would need negative luminance to hit 3:1, and X lighter than the text would need luminance ≥1.79 (above white, L=1.0) to hit 4.5:1 against the text. So no color satisfies both as literally specified while `#94a3b8` stays fixed on top of it.

Given that, `#506197` was chosen as the *optimal point on the feasible frontier*: among all backgrounds clearing the named WCAG 1.4.11 failure (≥3:1 vs ambient, the specific defect flagged for this fix), it sits at essentially the minimum luminance that still clears 3.00:1 — any dimmer and the primary defect isn't fixed, any brighter and text contrast only gets worse.
